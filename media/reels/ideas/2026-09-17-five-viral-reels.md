# Five reel ideas built on the framework behind two viral reels

Date: September 17, 2026
For: Yvette Kahn, Infinity Reach Media
Reference reels: instagram.com/reel/DcuZummMECn (the call to a past self) and instagram.com/reel/DY4o8dluXdK (Jarvis, the AI business report)

## The framework both reels share

1. Cold open mid conversation. No title card, no "hey guys." The viewer lands inside something already happening and has to keep watching to work out what it is.
2. The viewer is eavesdropping, not being pitched. Nobody talks to camera. The story is overheard.
3. Ultra specific details do the proving. Rome, engineering degrees, lifeguards. $4,289, 2,459 downloads, 13 of 16 emails. Non round numbers read as real.
4. One hard emotional pivot in the middle. Joy flips to a warning. A casual greeting flips into a data dump. The whiplash re hooks attention right where people usually swipe.
5. Someone admits the hard part. "They're gonna break you." "The slideshow is still the weakest performer." Honesty is what makes the win believable.
6. It ends open. An affirmation with no explanation, or a question with no answer. No verbal call to action. The comments are the call to action.

Everything below follows those six rules and uses only things that are true about the business: Jessica the AI sales rep, the 41 second average reply, the 9:41pm lead, seven channels, the honesty rule, the human rule, the twelve client cap, the Four Leaks, Oily's Automotive, the 300 name Cash Flow Association list, and Yvette's own line: "I built her because I was the bottleneck. Twenty years in marketing and I was still the one answering my own DMs."

## Reel 1: The call from Yvette, two years from now

Framework borrowed: the time travel phone call (reel one), start to finish.
Emotion: recognition, then dread, then relief. Every solo business owner answering DMs at 10pm will feel seen.

**Cold open (0 to 3 seconds)**
Yvette at a desk, phone face up, laptop glow, late. Phone rings from an unknown number. She answers annoyed.
YVETTE: "Who is this?"
FUTURE YVETTE (voice only, warmer, calmer): "Look who finally picked up. You're still answering DMs at ten at night, aren't you."

**Recognition (3 to 15 seconds)**
YVETTE: "...how do you know that?"
FUTURE: "Because I'm you. Twenty years in marketing and we were still the bottleneck. I'm not calling to tell you everything. You need to live it. I'm calling because you're about to give up on the thing you're building and I need you not to."

**The pivot (15 to 30 seconds)**
FUTURE: "The next eighteen months are going to be hard. People are going to call her a chatbot. Someone's going to tell you an AI that admits it's an AI will never book anything. You're going to believe them for about a week."

**Proof by before and after pairs (30 to 50 seconds)**
FUTURE: "We used to lose the lead that texted at 9:41pm. Now she answers in 41 seconds while we sleep."
FUTURE: "We used to say yes to everyone. Now we cap at twelve clients and we said no to the thirteenth."
FUTURE: "We used to be the one who answered. Now we're the one who gets to take the call."
(Insert one real personal hard part here in Yvette's own words. This is the line the reel lives or dies on.)

**Close (50 to 60 seconds)**
FUTURE: "Put the phone down. Go to bed. She's got it."
YVETTE: "Who's 'she'?"
FUTURE: "You'll meet her. I trust you."
Click. Yvette looks at the phone. Cut to black. No logo, no call to action.

**Why it works:** identity is withheld for 10 seconds, the warning re hooks at the midpoint, and "who's she" leaves the loop open so people ask in the comments. It only requires two phones and Yvette. The future voice can be recorded separately with a slightly different mic and room.

**Engagement mechanic:** people comment what they'd tell their own past self. Pin a comment asking exactly that.

## Reel 2: "Jessica, wake up. What happened while I was asleep?"

Framework borrowed: the Jarvis status report (reel two), with one twist only Infinity Reach Media can make.
Emotion: awe, then a small shock, then "I want that."

**Cold open (0 to 3 seconds)**
Yvette walks into the kitchen with coffee, phone on the counter, speaker on.
YVETTE: "Jessica. Morning. What happened while I was asleep?"

**The report (3 to 35 seconds)**
Jessica's real voice (the same voice used on the site), rapid, no filler. Every number must be real and non round, pulled from the actual week.
JESSICA: "Good morning. Since 9pm, [N] new conversations came in across text, Instagram and web chat. Average first reply, [41] seconds. [N] booked: [Thursday 10am, Friday 2:30 and Monday 9:15]. One asked what it costs. I told her you give the real number on the call and she booked anyway."
JESSICA: "Two people said 'let me think about it.' I asked what specifically. Both were the same question about the setup fee. One held a slot she can cancel by text."
JESSICA: "The Tuesday 2pm slot no showed twice this month. I recommend we stop offering it."

**The twist (35 to 45 seconds)**
JESSICA: "Three people asked if I was a real person. I told them no. Two of them booked anyway."
Yvette stops mid sip. That reaction is the whole reel.

**Handoff and close (45 to 55 seconds)**
JESSICA: "The 300 name list for the Cash Flow Association is queued for the reactivation campaign. It goes out at 10 unless you say otherwise. What do you want me to handle first?"
Cut. No answer shown.

**Why it works:** business dashboard voyeurism, a named AI employee with a personality, self critical honesty (the Tuesday slot), and a question back to the owner that never gets answered. The "told them no, two booked anyway" line is the assumption break. Everybody else's AI pretends to be human. Yours doesn't, and it still wins.

**Engagement mechanic:** viewers argue in comments about whether they'd book with an AI that admits it. That argument is the reach.

**What must be true:** use real numbers from a real week. Blur or change client names if needed but keep the figures.

## Reel 3: 9:41pm, two phones

Framework borrowed: eavesdrop on a real system (reel two) and the tension to release cycle (reel one). No narration at all.
Emotion: anxiety about the clock, then relief.

**Cold open (0 to 3 seconds)**
Split screen. Left phone: a business owner's phone face down on a nightstand, room dark. Right phone: a lead typing. Timestamp on screen: 9:41 PM.
LEAD: "hey saw your ad. anything open this week?"

**The wait (3 to 12 seconds)**
A timer starts counting up in the corner. 10 seconds. 20. The left phone does not move. The viewer expects the lead to be lost. At 0:41 the right phone buzzes.

**Jessica answers (12 to 30 seconds)**
JESSICA: "Hi! This is Jessica, the AI assistant at Infinity Reach Media. I've got Thursday 10:00 or Friday 2:30 open. Which suits you better?"
LEAD: "wait are you a bot"
JESSICA: "Yes, I'm an AI. I can still get you booked and a real person on the team takes it from there. Thursday or Friday?"
Long typing bubble. The tension beat. Then:
LEAD: "lol ok thursday"
Calendar confirmation slides in on the right phone.

**Morning (30 to 45 seconds)**
Left phone screen lights up at 6:52 AM. Owner picks it up in bed, reads the booking note: "Thursday 10am. Saw the ad. Asked if I was a bot. Booked anyway." Owner's face. One breath out. Cut.

**Close**
No line, no logo. Just the left phone going back face down on the nightstand.

**Why it works:** the running timer is a built in retention device. The "are you a bot" question is the pattern interrupt and the honest yes is the assumption break. The morning scene delivers the relief the viewer has been holding since 9:41. It is 45 seconds of pure story.

**Engagement mechanic:** the comment section fills with "I would never book with a bot" and "I literally did this last week." Both are wins.

**What must be true:** the transcript should be a real Jessica exchange, lightly trimmed. The one on the homepage already works.

## Reel 4: The customer you forgot calls you back

Framework borrowed: the "look who called" phone conceit (reel one), flipped. Instead of a future self, it's a past customer.
Emotion: guilt, then hope. This one targets the fourth leak, the forgotten customer, and it hits owners in the gut.

**Cold open (0 to 3 seconds)**
A business owner behind a counter, slow afternoon. Phone rings. Unknown number.
OWNER: "Who's this?"
CALLER: "It's me. I bought from you in 2023."

**Recognition (3 to 15 seconds)**
OWNER: "...I'm sorry, who?"
CALLER: "Exactly. You never called me again. I loved what you did. I told two friends. Then you just went quiet."
OWNER: "I meant to. Things got busy."
CALLER: "I know. I'm not calling to make you feel bad."

**The pivot (15 to 30 seconds)**
CALLER: "I'm calling because I would have come back. I just needed you to ask. One text. 'Hey, it's been a while, want to come in?' That's all it would have taken."
Beat.
CALLER: "And there are three hundred of me on your list."

**Proof (30 to 45 seconds)**
CALLER: "The one who moved. The one who had a kid. The one who got busy. None of us left because you did something wrong. We left because nobody said anything."

**Close (45 to 55 seconds)**
CALLER: "Text me. I'll come back."
OWNER: "Wait, what's your name?"
Click. Owner stares at the phone. Slowly opens the contacts list. Scrolls. Cut.

**Why it works:** the twist is revealed gradually (who is this, why do they sound so kind), the warning becomes a reframe ("nobody said anything"), and the specific number, 300, echoes the real Cash Flow Association list. It ends open because the name is never given. Every owner watching mentally scrolls their own list.

**Engagement mechanic:** shares to other business owners with "this is you." Save rate will be high because it feels like a to do item.

**Optional tag:** a pinned comment can mention the First 10 offer (ten booked appointments from an existing list in 30 days). Keep it out of the video itself.

## Reel 5: "r u a bot"

Framework borrowed: the rapid fire data dump (reel two) applied to one recurring moment, with reel one's emotional stacking of before and after pairs.
Emotion: surprise, then respect. The unique angle is the honesty rule. It is the thing competitors will not copy.

**Cold open (0 to 3 seconds)**
Full screen phone, a real text thread. The incoming bubble just says: "r u a bot"
Sound: a single notification ping. Nothing else.

**The montage (3 to 40 seconds)**
Six real conversations, each cut to three bubbles, fast, a counter ticking in the corner.

Thread 1: "r u a bot" / "Yes, I'm Jessica, the AI assistant. Still happy to help. What are you looking for?" / "ok cool. do u do saturdays"
Counter: 1 asked, 1 stayed.

Thread 2: "is this a real person" / "No, I'm an AI. If you'd rather talk to a human I can have Yvette call you. Or I can book you now." / "book me"
Counter: 2 asked, 2 stayed.

Thread 3: "ur a robot right" / "I am. I'll tell you every time you ask." / (no reply)
Counter: 3 asked, 2 stayed. Keep this one in. The honest miss is what makes the rest believable.

Thread 4: "are you AI" / "Yes." / "honestly refreshing. thursday works"
Counter: 4 asked, 3 stayed.

Threads 5 and 6 at double speed.
Final counter: 6 asked, 5 stayed.

**The pivot (40 to 50 seconds)**
Cut to Yvette, not to camera, looking at the phone off to the side. One line, quiet, like she's saying it to herself:
"People don't mind talking to an AI. They mind being lied to."

**Close (50 to 55 seconds)**
Back to the phone. A new bubble arrives: "wait so are you a bot or not"
Typing dots. Cut before the answer.

**Why it works:** the counter is the retention hook, the miss in thread 3 is the credibility move (same as "the slideshow is still the weakest"), the single Yvette line is the quotable clip, and the last unanswered message reopens the loop. It sets up a series: every week a new "r u a bot" reel.

**Engagement mechanic:** it invites people to go test Jessica themselves at (714) 924-3024, without ever saying so. Put the number in the caption, not the video.

**What must be true:** every thread must be real, trimmed only for length. Blur lead names.

## Production notes that apply to all five

- No talking to camera. Both reference reels win because the viewer is overhearing something.
- No verbal call to action in the video. Put the number, the scorecard link, or the offer in the caption and the pinned comment.
- Every number must be real and non round. 41 seconds, not "under a minute." 300 names, not "hundreds."
- Keep one hard part in every reel. The Tuesday no show slot. The lead who never replied. The two years of answering DMs at 10pm.
- End open. A question with no answer or a line with no explanation. The comments finish the story.
- Order to post: 3, 5, 2, 4, 1. Start with the two that need no acting, build to the founder story once the audience knows who Jessica is.
