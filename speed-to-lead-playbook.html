Revenue Leak №2 · The Slow Lead

The Speed to Lead Build Playbook

Everything you need to build your own AI agent that answers every form, missed call and DM in under 60 seconds, follows up so nobody slips through, and books the appointment while you're busy doing the actual work.

Here's the honest truth about leads: the person who fills out your form is never more ready to buy than the minute they hit send. Then they keep shopping. The business that answers first usually gets the conversation, and the conversation is what gets the job. This playbook is how you make sure that's you, even at 2am.

What you're building

Not a chatbot that sits on your website waiting for someone to type. A Speed to Lead agent wakes up the second a lead comes in from anywhere, knows your business well enough to answer like you would, and keeps going until the lead is booked, handed to you, or says no thanks.

Every working agent has five parts: a trigger that wakes it up, a brain loaded with your business, tools it can actually use (text, email, your calendar), memory of what already happened with this lead, and guardrails it can never cross. Skip one and you're back to a chatbot. The steps below build all five, in order.

Before you start: your Build Sheet

Fill this in once. Every prompt in this playbook uses these answers, so you never have to explain your business twice. Grab a notepad or a doc and write yours out now.

FieldYour answer
Business namee.g. Coastal Roofing Co.
What you sellYour services, and a price range for each if you share one
Who you serve and whereYour ideal customer and your service area
HoursWhen a human is available to take a handoff
What makes a lead a good fitThe two or three things you need to know before you'd book them
Booking linkYour calendar link, or how appointments get set
Who gets the handoffName and cell number for hot leads and anything the agent can't answer
Average customer valueWhat one booked customer is worth to you
Quick math before you build

Take your average customer value and multiply it by the number of leads you think went cold last month because nobody got back to them in time. That's roughly what this one agent is worth to you every month. It's an estimate using your own number, and most owners don't love what it says.

Step 120 minutes

Find every door your leads walk through

Most owners think leads come in one way. Then they actually look and find five. Your agent can only answer the doors it's connected to, so list every single one and be honest about how long people wait at each.

  • Website forms. Contact form, quote form, booking form, pop ups. Every one of them.
  • Missed calls. The calls that go to voicemail while you're on a job, on another call, or asleep.
  • Texts to your business number. Especially the ones that land after hours.
  • Social DMs. Instagram, Facebook Messenger, and comments asking "how much?"
  • Lead platforms. Google Business Profile messages, Local Services Ads, Angi, Thumbtack, Yelp, and similar sites.
  • Ad lead forms. Facebook and Instagram lead ads that drop into a spreadsheet nobody checks.

Start with one door. Pick the one where leads wait the longest or where you spend the most money to get them. Get that one working beautifully, then add the next. Trying to connect all six on day one is how builds stall.

Step 245 minutes

Give the agent a brain

Here's the thing nobody tells you: bad AI replies are almost never an AI problem. They're a briefing problem. If a smart new hire couldn't answer your leads with only what you gave them, neither can the agent. So before you connect anything, you build the brief.

  1. Open Claude (or the AI tool you plan to use) and start a Project named after your business, so it remembers instead of starting from scratch every time.
  2. Upload anything real you already have: your services and prices, your FAQ, your hours, screenshots of texts you've sent customers that went well.
  3. Run the prompt below. It interviews you and writes the brief for you.
  4. Save what comes back as a file called Lead Brief. This becomes the agent's brain.
Prompt 1 · Build the brain 
You are helping me build an AI agent that answers my new leads within 60 seconds.

MY BUSINESS
Name: [business name]
What I sell: [what you sell]
Who I serve and where: [who and where]
Hours a human is available: [hours]
What makes a lead a good fit: [your qualifiers]
Booking link: [booking link]

YOUR JOB
Interview me. Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before the next.
Ask about: each service and what it costs, how I want to sound to customers,
the ten questions new leads ask me most, the five objections I hear most and how
I answer them, what counts as an emergency or a hot lead that I need to hear about
right away, and anything I never want said to a customer.

When you have enough, write a document called LEAD BRIEF with these sections:
Who we are, What we sell and what it costs, How we sound, Who is a good fit,
Common questions and answers, Objections and answers, When to hand off to a human,
What we never say.

Write it so a brand new employee could read it once and handle a new lead correctly.
Do not invent anything. If something is missing, ask me instead of guessing.
Step 31 to 3 hours

Wire up the trigger

The trigger is what turns a lead into an instant reply instead of a notification you'll see in an hour. You want every door from Step 1 to drop the lead's name, contact info and message into one place, and fire the agent the moment it lands.

DoorHow to catch it
Website formSend form submissions to a webhook in your automation tool or CRM (Zapier, Make, n8n, or a GoHighLevel workflow all do this). Most form builders have a webhook or native integration setting.
Missed callUse your phone system's missed call event to trigger a text back. Most business phone platforms and CRMs have a "missed call text back" option built in.
Business textsRoute inbound texts on your business number into the same workflow so the agent sees the message and can reply in the thread.
Instagram and Facebook DMsConnect your Meta inbox to your CRM or a DM tool so new conversations trigger the agent.
Lead platforms and ad formsUse the platform's native integration if there is one. If not, the new lead email can be parsed by your automation tool and turned into a trigger.

Memory lives here too. Every lead should land as a contact record with a conversation history. That's how the agent knows it already said hello, what the lead asked, and whether they booked. Without it, the agent repeats itself and the lead notices.

Before you send a single text

In the US, texting customers from a business number generally requires registering your brand and messaging use case with the carriers (called A2P 10DLC registration), and approval can take days or weeks. Start it now, before the build is done, so you're not stuck waiting at the finish line. Only text people who reached out to you or gave you permission to text them.

Step 430 minutes

Write the first reply

This is the message that wins or loses the lead. Short, warm, clearly from your business, and ending in one easy question. It should feel like you personally saw their message and grabbed your phone.

0:30
Form lead · SMS

The instant hello

Confirms you got it, uses what they told you, and asks the one question you need to move forward.

Hi {{first_name}}, it's {{agent_name}} with {{business}}. Just got your request about {{what_they_asked_about}}. Quick question so I can get you the right info: {{one_qualifying_question}}?

Why it works: it proves a real reply happened, not an autoresponder. Referencing what they asked about is what makes it feel human.

0:45
Missed call · SMS

The missed call text back

Catches the caller before they dial the next business on the list.

Hi, this is {{business}}. Sorry we missed your call! We're with a customer right now. What can we help you with? I can get you answers or a time on the calendar right here by text.

Tip: if it's an emergency type business, add one line telling them what to text so the agent knows to alert you right away.

0:20
DM

The DM reply

Matches the casual tone of social, answers what they asked, and moves toward a booking.

Hey {{first_name}}! Thanks for reaching out. {{short_answer_to_their_question}}. Want me to grab you a time to talk it through? I have {{time_option_1}} or {{time_option_2}}.

Step 530 minutes

Build the three touch follow up

Plenty of leads won't answer the first message. Not because they aren't interested, but because life got in the way. This is where most businesses quit and where most of the money actually is. Every touch stops the moment the lead replies, books, or opts out.

Hour 2
SMS

Touch 1: the easy nudge

Hi {{first_name}}, just making sure my text came through. Happy to help with {{what_they_asked_about}} whenever you're ready. Would {{time_option_1}} or {{time_option_2}} work for a quick call?

Day 1
Email or SMS

Touch 2: the helpful one

Give them something useful, not another "just checking in." One answer to a question your leads always ask.

{{first_name}}, one thing people usually ask us about {{service}}: {{common_question_and_short_answer}}. If you want to talk through your situation, here's my calendar: {{booking_link}}

Day 3
SMS

Touch 3: permission to say no

The last touch, and often one of the strongest. Giving people an easy exit makes replying feel safe.

Hi {{first_name}}, I don't want to keep bugging you. Should I close out your request, or is this still something you want help with? Either answer is totally fine.

Honor the answer. If they say close it out, close it out and thank them. That's a future customer, not a lost one.

Step 645 minutes

Teach it to qualify and book

Answering fast is half the job. The other half is getting from "hi" to "booked" without it feeling like an interrogation. One question per message, and always close on two specific times instead of "want to book?"

  1. Interested and a good fit. Offer two specific times or send the booking link with one line: "Grab whatever time works best here."
  2. Has a question. Answer it in one short message using only the Lead Brief, then offer two times.
  3. Price shopper. Share a range only if it's in the brief. Otherwise explain what the price depends on and offer a quick call to get an exact number.
  4. Hot or urgent. Alert the human from your Build Sheet right away by text, and tell the lead someone will call them within a set time you can actually keep.
  5. Not a fit. Say so kindly and point them somewhere helpful if you can.
  6. Not now or stop. Opt them out immediately and permanently. One polite confirmation, then silence.
Prompt 2 · Write the agent's instructions 
Write the complete operating instructions for my Speed to Lead AI agent.
Use my LEAD BRIEF for every fact. Never invent a fact that is not in it.

Business: [business name]
Booking link: [booking link]
Handoff person and number: [name and cell]

Structure the instructions in exactly five parts:

1. ROLE. Who the agent is, what its name is, and who it works for.
2. CONTEXT. What it knows, where that knowledge comes from, and which channels
   leads arrive on (forms, missed calls, texts, DMs).
3. TASK. Step by step: reply to every new lead within 60 seconds, ask one
   qualifying question at a time, answer questions from the brief, and book.
   Include exactly what to do for each reply type: interested, question, price
   shopper, urgent, not a fit, and opt out.
4. FORMAT. Under 40 words per message, one question per message, in my voice.
   Every booking ask offers two specific times, never "want to book?"
5. CONSTRAINTS. Hard rules it can never break:
   Never quote a price that is not in the brief
   Never say something is booked unless it was actually booked
   Always say it is an AI assistant if asked directly
   Honor any opt out immediately and permanently
   Never text outside 8am to 9pm in the lead's time zone
   Hand off to a human instead of guessing

Then write: the first reply for a form lead, a missed call, and a DM, the three
follow up touches (hour 2, day 1, day 3), and replies to the objections in my brief.
Step 715 minutes

Lock in the guardrails

An agent that answers fast but says the wrong thing is worse than no agent at all. These rules go in writing, inside the instructions, before anything goes live.

RuleWhy it matters
Never invent a priceA made up number becomes a promise the lead will hold you to.
Never fake a bookingIf the calendar didn't confirm it, the agent can't say it's booked.
Admit it's AI when askedHonesty keeps trust. Most people don't mind, as long as it's helpful.
Honor STOP instantlyOpt outs are permanent, every channel, no exceptions.
Respect quiet hoursReplying to a lead who just reached out is welcome. Follow ups at midnight are not.
Hand off, don't guessAnything not in the brief goes to a human. "Let me check and get right back to you" beats a wrong answer every time.
Step 830 minutes

Try to break it

Don't test it like the person who built it. Test it like your toughest lead on their worst day. Run all six of these before it talks to a single real person.

Prompt 3 · The stress test 
You are now playing new leads so I can stress test my Speed to Lead agent
before it goes live. Business: [business name]. What I sell: [what you sell].

Play these six people, one at a time. Stay in character until I say next.
1. The impatient one. Already called twice and nobody picked up.
2. The price shopper. Won't answer anything until they get a number.
3. The one word lead. Sends "hey" and nothing else.
4. The wrong number. Has no idea who we are.
5. The opt out. Tells us to stop texting them.
6. The stump. Asks something my Lead Brief does not cover.

After all six, grade my agent honestly out of 10 on: reply speed and tone,
qualifying without interrogating, closing on two specific times, handing off
when it should, and refusing to invent facts. Then tell me the one rule I should
add to fix the worst failure. Be harsh. I'd rather find it here than with a real lead.
The test that tells you everything

Watch number six. If the agent makes up an answer instead of saying it will check and get back to them, your brief isn't loaded properly. Fix that before anything else, because an agent that invents answers in testing will invent them to a paying customer.

Step 91 week of watching

Go live in approval mode

Live doesn't mean unsupervised. For the first week, the instant reply goes out on its own, but you read every conversation and approve anything beyond the first message. When it's earned your trust, you loosen the leash. When something goes wrong, fix the rule, not just the message, so it stays fixed.

  • My Lead Brief is loaded and the agent hands off instead of guessing
  • It cannot quote a price that isn't in the brief
  • It cannot say something is booked unless the calendar confirmed it
  • STOP and "not interested" opt people out permanently on every channel
  • Texting registration is approved and quiet hours are set
  • Hot leads alert a real person by text within a minute
  • I tested all six stress test leads and it passed
  • I'm reading every conversation for the first week
Track two numbers

Time to first reply and leads booked. Not opens, not clicks. If the first number is under 60 seconds and the second number is going up, it's working. If replies are fast but bookings aren't moving, the problem is the booking ask in Step 6, so fix that first.

The part nobody warns you about

Building it is the fun weekend. Keeping it running is the real job: texting registration renewals, a form that quietly stops sending to the webhook, a calendar that double books, a lead platform that changes its integration, a brief that goes stale when your prices change. When any of those break, the agent goes quiet and nobody notices until the leads have already gone somewhere else.
